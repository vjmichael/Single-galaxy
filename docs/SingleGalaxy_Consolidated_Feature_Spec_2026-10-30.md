# Single Galaxy — Consolidated Feature Specification

**Revision:** 2026-10-30 · **Authority:** current design decisions · **Purpose:** concise single source of truth

**Implementation addendum (B7.0c):** mineral-specific geological mining, kilogram-level phase stock, refining into named goods and tailings, and alloy/aluminum construction modules are now implemented and Linux-tested. The B7.0b implementation table below is a historical baseline; see `B7_0c_Mineral_Industry_Report_2026-10-30.md` for the latest evidence. Agriculture, NPC needs, colony viability and managed absence are still design-only.  
**Product:** original, offline-capable space/colony survival-crafting, exploration, factory and community sandbox. EVE, The Sims, Minecraft, and other games are inspirations, not assets, protocols, maps, or mechanics to copy.  
**North star:** *Survival is the beginning; self-sufficiency creates freedom; thriving communities and player invention give the universe longevity.*

## 1. The player's starting world and core loop

- **Start:** exactly **one colony ship** carrying a **finite initial NPC crew**. Current test fixture uses **24 crew**, not a finalized balance decision. No starting stations, stargates, settlements, mines, factories, markets, trade routes, or other infrastructure. The ship supplies the initial exploration and essential industrial bootstrap capabilities; exact starting equipment/reserves remain to be designed.
- **Core loop:** fly and scan → discover a deposit → mine or farm → process/refine → make **usable** goods → build/operate/repair something → meet colonists' needs → delegate repeatable work → explore, trade, construct, or enjoy community life. Salvage, combat, and commerce create alternative paths.
- **No arbitrary player levels, ship unlocks, skill-point queues, or technology-tier grind.** Progress comes from things players can obtain, learn, invent, operate, cooperate on, and maintain. Equipment and engineering constraints still matter.
- **Real consequences:** people, machines, and physical stock are not automatically restored after mistakes. Colony-ship destruction can destroy refining/manufacturing capability; NPC deaths can be permanent. Recoveries require actual salvage, supplies, help, or rebuilding. Saving protects committed progress from crashes, not every player choice.
- **Player freedom:** hands-on Wing Commander-style flight, optional assists and **legitimate player autopilot**. The separate QA-only input automation is a development tool, not player autopilot. Players may specialize in exploration, farming, mining, industry, shipping, trading, combat, socializing, or governance without mandatory micromanagement.

## 2. Space, exploration, and player-built infrastructure

- **Continuous shared 3D physical space:** a ship can fly directly between stars without any stargate. Invisible streaming sectors and origin-rebased local physics are engine optimizations, not walls or teleports. A high-speed interstellar drive is needed; detailed propulsion and flight-time balance are open.
- **Astronomy-informed generation:** real stellar/exoplanet/asteroid data may establish positions, motion, or plausible physical attributes. Clearly tag **observed data** separately from fictional/procedural completions. Persist celestial bodies and discoveries.
- **Life-giving geography:** near the fiction's hospitable origin regions, viable settlement sites are more common; harsh remote regions are less naturally supportive, with **rare small oasis systems**. Planet/moon access commonly makes food, water and habitation easier than an asteroid-only site, but no location provides free infrastructure or guaranteed habitability. Rich minerals and life-support potential are separate properties.
- **Player construction is universal:** players choose sites and construct **all functional infrastructure**—outposts, farms, greenhouses, refineries, mines, factories, shipyards, stations, logistics hubs, habitats, roads/corridors, and **eventual optional stargates**. Existing Lumen Reach stations/gates are *developer-only test fixtures*, not world-start lore. Other players can contribute materials, labor, and upkeep to shared structures.
- **Exploration/knowledge:** directional sensors, deployable probes, geological, tactical, planetary/moon surveying, mapping, timestamps, confidence, stale observations, and public/private shared discoveries. Sensors expose observations, not omniscient world truth. Frontier regions can offer unexploited opportunities without a magical rule that rare elements increase with distance.
- **Security and sovereignty emerge** from people, organizations, defended infrastructure and logistics. Shorter dangerous routes, safer longer routes, patrols, piracy, trade, conflicts and discovered resource locations create geographical choices.

## 3. Physical production: minerals into useful goods

- **Large finite asteroids** have bulk mass, geological composition, accessible reserves and depletion. Mineral types and proportions are *plausible game models* unless explicitly measured; deposits do not respawn. Real mineral names and useful byproducts are encouraged.
- **Aluminum is our Tritanium analogue:** geologically common in this fictional universe, **cheap and efficient to process by game rules**, low unit value, and indispensable in very high volumes for structures, ships, housing and industrial replacement. Iron–nickel, silicon, copper, water, magnesium, and specialized materials have distinct roles; rare resources unlock possibilities, not a compulsory tier treadmill.
- **End-to-end resource chains:** ore and mineral fractions → separated concentrates → refined materials and byproducts/tailings → components → **functioning** equipment, structures, consumer goods and services. Energy, throughput, storage, labor, waste and recycling matter, with transparent conservation accounting and practical simplifications. A completed recipe must *do something*, not only produce a higher-tier icon.
- **Agriculture is a first-class activity:** farms/greenhouses, crops, meals and variety, water, nutrients, food processing, preservation, delivery and agricultural trade. People eat; food quality and availability affect living conditions. Mining and farming are complementary viable professions.
- **Industry and logistics:** local ownership/storage, transport, contracts, manufacturing, salvage/repair, recycling, shipbuilding, automation, maintenance, and cargo accounting. Items do not teleport between stations. Supply/demand and recurring consumption sustain ongoing production even after a colony achieves sufficiency.
- **Composable building blocks:** modular structures, machines, power, pipes, conveyors, controllers, drones, sensors, permissions, contracts and automation enable player inventions the developers did not foresee. No prescribed final factory or civilization blueprint.

## 4. People: workforce, living needs, and relationships

- **NPCs are persistent persons, not spawned labor tokens.** The founding population is finite. They follow the same resource, equipment, location, injury, employment and economic rules as players; individuals can work, trade, fight, leave, form businesses or settlements, and die.
- **Work and skill:** a new competent NPC has roughly an average player's base productivity with equal tools. NPCs develop **job-specific experience through work**; veteran proficiency matters. A higher-skilled mentor accelerates a lower-skilled apprentice's job XP while the apprentice works, ending at proficiency parity. NPC managers enable delegated operations.
- **Life simulation (later):** personal preferences, families, friendships, romance, marriage, children, generational growth, faster maturation and slower aging at game-scaled rates. Population depends on genuine resources and circumstances, not arbitrary spawning. Divorce, inheritance, courts, and even marriage litigation may emerge from the institutions players choose to create.
- **Needs and wants:** nutrition, rest, social connection and recreation are the initial proposed meters; comfort, safety, health, ambitions and personality add depth later. NPCs autonomously pursue available ways to satisfy needs—players build the facilities and society, not click every meal. Good housing, food variety, leisure, meaningful work and community make settlement worth living in.
- **Cumulative grievance and disposition:** repeated unmet needs/injustice increase a persistent *unhappiness/grievance* meter through **content → quiet quitting → aggrieved → disrespectful → defiant**. Resentment/trust outlast momentary hunger; repaired conditions and fair treatment can reduce grievances over time. Quiet quitting can reduce discretionary effort and mentoring, including among experts.
- **Defiant is not automatically violent:** disaffected NPCs may organize, petition, strike, negotiate reforms, secede with real assets, or, if circumstances escalate, attempt political overthrow or conflict. Groups form from shared interests, bonds and leaders. No instant sabotage or scripted rebellion for missing a five-second dialogue choice.
- **Player-built institutions**—clinics, recreation centers, schools, labor councils, mediation, courts, public services, and governance—have actual effects on welfare, trust, opportunity and dispute resolution. Institutions can constrain their founders; outcomes depend on rules, people, and resources rather than flat happiness bonuses.

## 5. Viability, progress, economy, and managed absence

- **Colony development is not automatic time-based leveling.** It reflects material and social capacity: **survival → stability → livability → self-sufficiency → prosperity → civilization**. These are *descriptive overlapping conditions*, **not hard technology locks**. A fragile or impoverished colony may be unable to sustain new construction even if the player can attempt it.
- **Progression/viability assessment:** evaluate sustained food, water, oxygen, power, housing, health, rest and leisure; supply buffers and repair capacity; staff/time available beyond essential work; production surplus; transport access; and NPC satisfaction/grievances. Use trends and bottleneck explanations instead of one magic happiness number. A colony needs quality of life **above mere survival** and resilience before managers responsibly undertake autonomous expansion.
- **Poverty is a real constraint, not a punishment timer:** NPCs in survival conditions prioritize essentials, repairs, trade and stabilization. A stable, well-run settlement can remain stable indefinitely. No forced resource exhaustion, scripted migration, endless disasters, or escalating mandatory chores once a problem is solved.
- **Managed absence is a core architectural requirement:** private/offline servers support NPC delegation, standing budgets/policies, autonomous agriculture/industry/repairs, and efficient offscreen simulation while players explore or stop playing. **No inactivity tax, daily-login pressure, or automatic ruin merely for logging out.** Economic autonomy requires actual supplies, skills and resilience; missing inputs cannot be conjured by time skips.
- **Returning players:** concise optional history/return briefing; NPC communities may have grown, changed relationships, formed institutions, negotiated independence or elected new leadership. Returning founders can resume adventuring without being required to reclaim control. Server owners configure pause/offline clocks and protection/relationship rules; details remain undecided.
- **Economy and social goals drive play:** useful goods and services answer real needs and chosen wants—food today, a comfortable home, an exploration ship, a trading business, reliable infrastructure—not simply a linear chain of resource tiers. Player institutions, NPC enterprise and logistics should produce open-ended emergent play.

## 6. What's implemented versus still design-only

| Area | Verified prototype as of B7.0b (Linux) | Future design / missing piece |
|---|---|---|
| Core simulation | Deterministic bounded **2D** ship/mine simulation; SQLite persistence, recovery, authoritative commands | Continuous **3D** galaxy; streamed interstellar flight and robust private-server world lifecycle |
| Colony start | One ship, simulated **24-person crew count**, no prebuilt infrastructure; scan/mine/refine/fabricate/build first owned outpost | Individual NPCs, true onboard starter inventory and complete bootstrap economy |
| Asteroids | Three million-tonne asteroid bodies, persisted mass and **illustrative** named-mineral profiles | Mineral-specific mining cargo, separation, actual usable refining outputs, heterogeneous geological layers |
| Interface | Matrix Vision local controls, validated telemetry; Linux protocol/relay tests | Actual graphical Chromium playthrough (blocked in test environment), native Windows validation, polished interface |
| Navigation/gates | Separate **prebuilt fixture** Lumen Reach: seven systems, gate routes, outage/maintenance/recovery, QA tests | No built-in gates in released starting world; player-built gate creation, legitimate autopilot, continuous world |
| Economy/community | Simplified generic ore → modules → outpost; earlier fixture docking, market, logistics | Agriculture, physical material recipes, NPC identities/needs, viability/progression, independent societies, autonomous colony management |

**Recorded B7.0b evidence:** 29/29 native/integration suites and 50/50 Node tests passed on Linux in bounded runs; Windows-native game and actual graphical Chromium playtest **not verified**. The existing 21-sheet economy workbook is a **design model**, not executable game logic. Historical milestone reports remain evidence of what each build actually tested.

## 7. Practical development order

1. **Delivered in B7.0c:** mineral-specific extraction, separated inventories, refining into usable material stock and physical tailings, functional structural modules and outpost construction, with SQLite/recovery testing. Full recycling, other functional goods and complex equipment remain upcoming.
2. **Core-loop expansion:** add farms, food and water processing, life-support consumption, basic NPC work assignments, and the first readable nutrition/rest/social/recreation needs. Build a tiny self-sufficient settlement in a test scenario.
3. **Viability and delegation:** implement an explainable colony progress assessment, maintenance and labor bottlenecks, simple NPC management policies, and managed-absence simulation with no magical growth.
4. **Spatial/creative foundation:** prove gate-free continuous 3D flight and streaming; develop modular player construction, sensors, logistics and ordinary player autopilot. Migrate fixture systems only when the new space model passes tests.
5. **Later depth:** experience/mentorship, broader economy and trade, social dispositions and institutions, NPC families/factions/secession, property law and legal drama, diplomacy, meaningful PvE/PvP and multiplayer/private-server governance.

**Decisions still open:** final starting crew/equipment quantities; food and agriculture recipes; pacing and birth/aging rates; interstellar propulsion; exact resource/generation rules; NPC grievance thresholds; legal/autonomy policy; offline simulation scaling; and private-server protections. None should be silently treated as finalized because the user says “proceed.”
